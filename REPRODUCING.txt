USING GAME THEORY TO DETECT FRAUD IN AI-ASSISTED BANKING — REPRODUCTION AND SCOPE
Revised 26 October 2026

This is a theoretical working paper with completed synthetic computations.
It contains no live language-model experiments, observed bank transactions,
measured fraud reductions, or evidence of deployment readiness. Its proposed
live-agent evaluation is explicitly future work. Nariman Orujov prompted
this work; all other aspects of the work and paper preparation were carried
out by AI agents. No affiliation, publication, or peer-review status is asserted.

DEPENDENCIES
Python 3.12+ (the recorded run used 3.14.4), NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.17.1,
Matplotlib 3.10.7, and PyMuPDF 1.28.0 (metadata removal). The supplied
environment used Matplotlib's distribution build 3.10.7+dfsg1. Exact numerical certificates have explicit tolerances;
minor last-bit differences across architectures/solver builds are possible.
The dependency pins are in analysis/requirements.txt.
PDF builds need pdflatex and bibtex. Artifact verification also needs
pdftotext (Poppler). The original small calculation needs only the standard library
for numbers, Matplotlib for plots, and PyMuPDF to clean PDF metadata.

EXPERIMENT DESIGN
100 evidence graphs, seeds 20261026 through 20261125 inclusive.
Each has 6 routes, 5 evidence sources, 7 checks; source map (0,0,1,1,2,3,4).
Every route has 2 or 3 randomly selected eligible sources. Route losses are
integers 30 through 100. Initial reveal probabilities are uniform [.65,.95).
Budgets 1 and 2 and disclosure probabilities 0, .25, .5 give 600 settings.
Seven policies give 4,200 rows in all_policy_results.csv.

Three design scenarios:
  nominal: common failure .03, initial reveal probabilities;
  A: common failure .15, source 0/1 reveal probabilities multiplied by .35;
  B: common failure .15, source 2/3/4 reveal probabilities multiplied by .35.
Held-out loss-matrix mixture: .2 nominal + .4 A + .4 B.
Omitted adverse scenario: common failure .35, source 0/2 initial reveal
probabilities multiplied by .15. No policy is fitted to that scenario. An
outside-scenario oracle is stored only as a diagnostic.

The source-aware model collapses repeated access to the SAME reveal outcome.
It does not assert that every second inspection of a document is useless.
A source's outcome can differ from a second inspection if the latter reveals
new information; those must be modeled as separate, dependent outcomes.

Finite-sample study: 100 graphs x 3 sample sizes (50, 200, 1000) at budget 2
and disclosure .25. Each of 522 cells has independent synthetic Bernoulli
outcomes scaled by route loss. Binomial counts implement those observations.
No model APIs or bank data are used. Seed = 20261026 + 100000 + graph*10000 + n.
The 95% bound is simultaneous WITHIN each generated loss matrix; the observed
100/100 coverage at each n is descriptive, not a claim of universal coverage.

VERIFICATION
The extended run solves 4,523 defender LPs and their attacker duals, checks
probability feasibility, exhaustively enumerates source states in a reference
case, checks alias invariance, verifies the alias counterexample for budgets
1 through 6, compares 103 disclosure-grid values with exact formulas, and
checks perturbation and regret bounds. summary.json records maximum errors.

Each graph contributes six related settings. The paper does not treat these
as 600 independent bank observations or attach empirical bank-risk intervals.
Most comparisons use exact expectations under known synthetic inputs; an
optimizer's superiority on its own objective is expected by construction.
The omitted-scenario losses and nominal cost of robustness are reported.
